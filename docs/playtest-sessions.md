# Moderated playtest sessions

The first playtest of this game was simulated: five personas, played out on paper. This guide is
for checking those findings with real people. Plan 5 to 8 sessions for each persona type below,
about 40 minutes each, one player at a time, on the player's own phone if they have one.

| Persona | Who to recruit | What we're checking |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The disengaged skeptic | Low trust in government, skims, little patience for text | Does Quick play hold them? Do they trust the numbers once they see the sources? |
| 2. The idealistic activist | Cares a lot about one cause, frustrated by trade-offs | Do the two-way cards and "who benefits" feel fair, or like the game picks sides? |
| 3. The pragmatic optimizer | Plays for points, treats it as a puzzle | Do the star thresholds and tie-breaks push them toward any one policy? |
| 4. The partisan hardliner | Strong views left or right | Does the game punish their views? Do party labels and votes read as neutral facts? |
| 5. The casual learner | Some civic knowledge, wants a story | Are the desk's gauges and the tap-to-define terms clear without help? |

Recruit across age, region and party. Pay for the time if you can, the same amount for everyone.
Don't recruit anyone who works on the game.

## Before the session: consent

Read this aloud, then ask for a yes. Note the yes in the session sheet; there's nothing to sign.

> Thanks for helping. You'll play a game about the federal budget for about half an hour, then
> we'll talk for ten minutes. We're testing the game, not you: there are no wrong answers, and
> getting stuck tells us what to fix.
>
> I'll take notes on what you do and say. I won't record video or audio unless you say yes to that
> separately. Your notes get a number, not your name. I won't ask how you vote or which party you
> belong to, and you don't have to share any view you'd rather keep to yourself.
>
> The game itself counts a few things for everyone who plays, as daily totals only: how many
> people open and finish each budget desk, how long the ripple round takes, how many answer the
> polls, and how many jump ahead. Those totals never include who you are.
>
> You can stop at any time, skip any question, or ask me to delete your notes later.
>
> Is that OK with you?

## The session (40 minutes)

Hand over the phone with the game's home screen open. Say as little as possible while they play.
If they ask what to do, say "What would you try?" Note the time at each stage.

1. **First look (3 min).** "Tell me what you think this is." Note what they tap first.
2. **Mission 1.1 (5 min).** Let them play it through. Note whether they read Uncle Sam's lines or
   skip them.
3. **A lesson mission, their choice of path (8 min).** Open mission 1.6. Point out Quick play only
   if they haven't found it after a minute. Note which path they take and whether the ripple round
   makes sense ("Which of these hits the economy least? Why?").
4. **The first budget desk, 1.9 (10 min).** "Uncle Sam wants the debt to stop outgrowing the
   economy. Have a go." Don't help. Note:
   - whether they find the cards' flip side, the "i" beside the economy gauge, and Hint;
   - whether the effect shown after playing a card surprises them;
   - when they get stuck, and whether they give up (that's the desk abandonment the game counts).
5. **The feed (4 min).** "Have a look around the Feed." Note whether they answer a poll, follow a
   bill, or tap a dotted term.
6. **Free play (5 min).** "Do whatever you like." Note whether they jump ahead to later missions.

## After play: questions (10 min)

Ask in this order, and write down their words, not your summary.

1. In one sentence, what was the game about?
2. Was there a moment you wanted to stop? What was happening?
3. Did the game seem to want you to choose a particular kind of plan? Which, and what made you
   think so?
4. Did anything seem slanted toward one party or one side? Where?
5. Were there words or numbers you didn't understand? Did you tap any of them?
6. On the budget desk, what did the "Economy" gauge tell you? And "Who pays"?
7. Would you play again tomorrow? Why or why not?
8. Rate it from 1 to 5: fun, fair, clear, and "I learned something."

## Session sheet

One row per session: session number, persona type, date, device, consent (yes, and whether audio
was allowed), minutes per stage, the stuck points, the answers above, and the four ratings. Keep
the sheet apart from any contact details used to schedule sessions, and delete those once the
round is done.

## Reading the results

Put the sheets next to the game's own counts on the stats page (`#stats`, the "How people play"
section, which also downloads as CSV): desk abandonment, ripple-round time, poll participation,
free-roam use and jump-ahead. A finding counts when at least three players of a type show it, or
when the counts move the same way.
